A board game:
from idea to print run

A good topic does not become a game on its own. You need a clear player experience, a working mechanic, tested content, a coherent visual system and production discipline.

Anton Konnov · 9 August 2026 · 8 minutes

Before the mechanics

Decide why people will play

It is better to start not with a board and cards, but with the audience, the situation and the desired experience. An entertainment game for a group, a corporate educational format and an informative product about a region all require different solutions.

At the outset, record the project objective, game conditions, number of participants, acceptable complexity and the action that should bring enjoyment or value.

Eight stages

How a concept becomes a product

01

Objective and audience

Who plays, in what situation, how long a session lasts and what experience the participant should gain.

02

Content

Gathering, verifying and adapting facts, stories, terms and source materials for the game format.

03

Game loop

Player objective, available decisions, consequences, player interaction and the condition for ending a game.

04

Paper prototype

A minimal set without expensive finishing allows you to check the mechanic itself quickly.

05

Testing

Sessions with different groups reveal unclear rules, dragging turns, dominant strategies and weak decisions.

06

Design

Information hierarchy, visual language, board, cards, components, rules and packaging all work as a single system.

07

Production proofs

Sizes, materials, bleeds, colour, stamps, completeness and technical checks of files for the chosen manufacturer.

08

Sample and print run

Before serial production, check colour, assembly, readability, packaging and the full composition of the physical product.

What to check

A test is observation, not a poll

Understanding

Can a player start without constant explanations from the designer?

Choice

Do decisions have clear consequences and several meaningful options?

Pacing

Where does waiting arise and does the duration match the promised format?

Replayability

Is there room for a new game after the player has encountered the content?

Product economics

Components define more than the look of a game

Every additional deck, miniature, finishing detail or non-standard box affects unit cost, minimum order quantity, lead times and logistics. The product composition is therefore refined alongside the mechanic, not after the design is complete.

For a custom project it is important to decide in advance whether a prototype, a limited corporate run or a product for regular sales is needed. In practice, the economically justified print run for a board game starts at 100 copies.
